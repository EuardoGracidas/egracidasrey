Brief profile

I am a Biomedical Engineer from Puebla, Mexico, currently pursuing a Master’s degree in Computer Science at Memorial University of Newfoundland. I began my undergraduate studies in Biomedical Engineering at Universidad de las Américas Puebla (UDLAP) in 2022, and from very early on I got involved in research, academic events and student competitions such as EXPOCEM at UDLAP, SILLONDROMO at the Instituto Nacional de Astrofísica, Óptica y Electrónica (INAOE), CNIB 2025 and ICOKG 2025, where my work received several awards and recognitions.

In parallel with my university studies, I completed professional certifications and specialized courses through institutions such as the University of Toronto, IEEE, Nature Masterclasses and Boston Scientific, as well as a Diploma in Neurolearning from Universidad Anáhuac.

After completing my research thesis in 2026, I graduated with honours, receiving the distinction of summa cum laude. During the same period, I published a scientific article in Biology, an MDPI journal (Q1; Impact Factor: 4.3; CiteScore: 7.3), presenting the research I conducted at INAOE during my social service and professional internship. Shortly afterwards, I was awarded a fully funded scholarship to pursue an M.Sc. in Computer Science at Memorial, where I began my graduate studies in September 2026. This transition allows me to bring together biomedical engineering, scientific research and advanced training in computer science.

Research

My research sits at the intersection of biology, engineering and computing. Most of my undergraduate work focused on the Madagascar hissing cockroach (Gromphadorhina portentosa) as a biological platform for biorobotic systems: building controlled-environment instrumentation, tracking locomotion with computer vision, and modelling how temperature, light and electrical stimulation shape the insect’s behaviour.

Medical device

Semi-portable Smart Pillbox for improving medical adherence in Polypharmacy Patients

Patients who take many medications at once often miss or mix up doses. This semi-portable pillbox addresses that with an automatic dispensing mechanism with 21 compartments, audiovisual alarms and a TFT display for real-time monitoring. Cyborg Insects Initiative (C2I)

Insects are renowned for their superior biomechanical capabilities compared to their robotic counterparts, which are often inefficient in chaotic environments and are more costly and challenging to produce. The Cyborg Insects Initiative (C2I) is a project in which I am engaged, focusing on the development of efficient biobots inspired by insects.

To achieve this objective, current research primarily involves characterizing various insect species under different environmental conditions, as well as analyzing their physiological activity using specialized instruments. Please feel free to contact me if you are interested in contributing to this initiative or wish to learn more about this research field.

Current advances

01

Machine learning for improving cockroach locomotion

We are developing efficient machine learning algorithms for strategically stimulating the cyborg cockroaches, so they do not become habituated to the stimulus and their movement can be controlled efficiently.

02

QR detection for motion tracking using videogrammetry

We developed a videogrammetry method for tracking the cockroach’s motion using only a webcam and a QR code. This lets us analyze its movement without adding a heavy electronic backpack or using complex tracking systems.

03

Efficient stimulation

We have put a lot of effort into characterizing Gromphadorhina portentosa behaviour under different stimulation parameters, to find the signal types that give the most efficient movement control.

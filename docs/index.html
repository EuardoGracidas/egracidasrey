Brief profile

I am a Biomedical Engineer currently pursuing a Master’s degree in Computer Science at Memorial University of Newfoundland. I was born in Puebla, Mexico, in 2004, and began my undergraduate studies in Biomedical Engineering at Universidad de las Américas Puebla (UDLAP) in 2022. Throughout my bachelor’s degree, I actively participated in a wide range of research projects, academic events, and competitions, including EXPOCEM, SILLONDROMO at the Instituto Nacional de Astrofísica, Óptica y Electrónica (INAOE), CNIB 2025, and ICOKG 2025, where I received several awards and recognitions. In parallel with my university studies, I completed several professional certifications and specialized courses through institutions such as the University of Toronto and Boston Scientific, as well as a Diploma in Neurolearning from Universidad Anáhuac.

After completing my research thesis in 2026, I graduated with honours, receiving the distinction of summa cum laude. During the same period, I successfully published a scientific article in Biology, an MDPI journal (Q1; Impact Factor: 4.3; CiteScore: 7.3), presenting the research I conducted at INAOE as part of my social service and professional internship. Shortly after completing my bachelor’s degree, I was awarded a fully funded scholarship to pursue a Master’s degree in Computer Science at Memorial University of Newfoundland, where I began my graduate studies in September 2026. This academic transition has allowed me to further integrate my background in biomedical engineering and scientific research with advanced training in computer science.

Cyborg Insects Initiative (C2I)

Insects are renowned for their superior biomechanical capabilities compared to their robotic counterparts, which are often inefficient in chaotic environments and are more costly and challenging to produce. The Cyborg Insects Initiative (C2I) is a project in which I am engaged, focusing on the development of efficient biobots inspired by insects. To achieve this objective, current research primarily involves characterizing various insect species under different environmental conditions, as well as analyzing their physiological activity using specialized instruments. Please feel free to contact me if you are interested in contributing to this initiative or wish to learn more about this research field.

Current advances

Machine learning for improving cockroach locomotion

We are developing efficient machine learning algorithms for strategically stimulating cyborg cockroaches to avoid habituation and achieve efficient movement control.

QR detection for motion tracking using videogrammetry

We developed a videogrammetry method for tracking cockroach motion using only a webcam and a QR code, so movement can be analyzed without a heavy electronic backpack or complex systems.

Efficient stimulation

We have characterized Gromphadorhina portentosa behaviour under different signal stimulation parameters to identify the most efficient signal types for movement control.
